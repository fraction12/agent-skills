# Feature Process

Use this for feature ideas, UX improvements, experiments, refactors with product impact, and vague product thoughts.

## Goal

Move from proposed solution to underlying user job, then to researched options, selected direction, and product-shaped spec.

## Intake Questions

Ask only what unlocks the next step. Prefer one question at a time. For each question, provide your recommended answer and wait for feedback before continuing when human judgment is needed.

High-value questions:

- Who is the user?
- What are they trying to get done?
- What pain or opportunity triggered this?
- What do they do today?
- Why is the current workflow insufficient?
- What would success look like?
- What must not change?
- Is this for MVP, power users, admins, internal ops, or everyone?

## Grilled Intent Rule

Phase 1 should feel like a serious interview. Do not move on until the real user/job, pain, desired outcome, assumptions, constraints, and success signal are understood. If the answer exists in the repo, docs, or web, inspect instead of asking.

## Product Shaping Checklist

After research and direction selection, clarify:

- chosen approach
- primary user
- primary job
- MVP scope
- non-goals
- core flow
- secondary flows
- roles/permissions
- data needed
- integrations/dependencies
- important edge cases
- success metrics
- rollout notes

## Product Shape Grilling

Before finalizing Product Shape, grill the important boundaries: why this MVP, why these non-goals, why this primary flow, and what tradeoffs are accepted.

## Feature Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- accepting the requested feature as the real problem
- asking a giant generic PM questionnaire
- deciding solution before research
- skipping non-goals
- vague tasks like “build feature”
- calling it final before UX and engineering fit are done
